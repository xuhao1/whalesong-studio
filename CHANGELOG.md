# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-07

First release, for DeepSeek Harness 0.2.0-rc.2.

### Added

- **Roles.** A showrunner agent preset (小说家 Novelist) that plans and coordinates, and four model roles it — or the author — calls: writer, architect, analyst and director, each routed to any model configured in the Harness.
- **Projects.** A book as a plain directory: `novel.json`, `outline.json`, a Markdown story bible (characters with structured personas, ranked world entries, style profiles), one Markdown file per chapter, and machine memory under `.novel/`.
- **Pipelines.** Foundation from an idea; chapter and beat derivation; what-if branches; drafting with automatic continuation; passage, chapter and book-wide revision as reviewable proposals; chapter digests with arc summaries; editorial review; bible expansion; style learning and sampling; import of existing manuscripts; export to Markdown, text, HTML and EPUB.
- **Memory and context.** Per-chapter digests (facts, character states, entities, thread movements), suggestions for the bible and character lifecycles, a context packer that switches between whole-manuscript and layered retrieval, and hybrid search (BM25 with CJK bigrams, optional embeddings). The context budget is 500,000 tokens by default and is held to 70 % of what the model can read; a role can state its model's window when nobody else knows it.
- **Novel Studio.** A page in the left sidebar and a per-session tab in the right sidebar with eight views: overview, bible, outline, write, read, cast, review, stage. Live streaming of drafts, selection-based revision, diffs, versions, search and find.
- **Stage.** Illustrations embedded in the text; audio drama with one voice per character, delivery directions and procedurally synthesized ambience; motion comics; playable interludes that can be discarded, kept as side stories or novelized into chapters.
- **One look per book, nothing hidden.** An art direction per book that the first picture writes into `novel.json` (from the Studio-wide direction or the built-in one) and that is shown with its origin wherever pictures are made; avoid lists per Studio, book, character and call; a visual anchor per character, distilled for an image model from the prose of the card; character design sheets (front, side, back), further looks (outfits) with sheets derived from the main one, and reference art for places — all sent to the image model as reference images where it takes them; and a record on every picture of what its prompt was assembled from.
- **Providers.** Speech: Bailian audio generation (`qwen-audio-3.1-tts-next`, with one reference clip per voice so a character sounds the same throughout), Qwen Omni realtime (WebSocket), DashScope Qwen-TTS with voice design, OpenRouter, OpenAI-compatible. Images: OpenRouter (GPT Image 2.5 and others), OpenAI-compatible, DashScope multimodal generation, and the GPT Image of a ChatGPT subscription through the Codex Connect plugin or the Codex CLI. Embeddings: OpenAI-compatible (Bailian, OpenRouter, OpenAI).
- **Setup.** A first-run guide and one-click setup per service (Aliyun Bailian, OpenRouter, OpenAI, ChatGPT subscription): paste one key and writing models, images, speech and retrieval are configured as recommended. Every slot can then name its own service, endpoint, key, model and reasoning level, choosing from a list of everything the endpoint serves (for speech: every model that can speak, with the protocol that drives it). Text roles may use any provider the Harness already reaches — keyed or signed in, such as a ChatGPT subscription through Codex Connect — or call any OpenAI-compatible endpoint directly.
- **Keys.** Typed into the settings and saved with them, into the Harness credential store — one record per endpoint host, shared by every slot on that service, never written to the settings file and never sent back to the page. The usual environment variables are picked up by themselves.
- **Books in.** Import of `.txt`, `.md`, `.epub` and `.docx` from the entry page (drag and drop) or from the conversation, and creation of a new Harness workspace on the spot.
- **The book in play.** A conversation is on one book at a time and the Studio beside it shows the same one. Changing book on either side moves both; the showrunner is told, in the conversation's own record, which book is in play and which one earlier turns were about. Every tool takes an optional `book` to touch another one once, results say which book they are about once a conversation has been on several, and a call always acts on the book its model was last told about.
- **Usage and cost.** A ledger of every model call the Studio makes — tokens per role, pictures, speech — summed by model, role, book and day, and costed at the unit prices the author enters. Also available to the showrunner (`novel_settings action=usage`).
- **Said, not swallowed.** Work that needs images or speech before they are set up, or that fails for a missing key or sign-in, opens a prompt that leads to the one-click setup or to the right section of the settings — for jobs nobody was waiting on too. Voicing a chapter shows the lines done, the line in the booth, the time taken and the time left.
- **One page to read and to listen.** The reader shows a chapter two ways: as prose, and as an audio drama with the sentence being spoken lit in the text, any sentence a place to start from, and chapters following one another. The author's production desk for the audio version sits above the same page.
- **On a phone.** The reader can be served to other devices on the local network: off until switched on for a book, read-only, behind an address with a random token that can be replaced, shown as a link and as a code to scan.
- **One voice, one level.** A voice sheet per book so every speaker — with a card or without — keeps one voice across chapters and interludes; a listening check on every synthesized line (cut short, silent, padded, or not in its voice) with automatic retakes; all lines brought to one loudness; scripts that keep narration whole and say so when a passage could not be directed.
- **No cap of the Studio's own on what a model writes.** A model that reasons spends its reasoning out of the same allowance as its answer; replies run as long as the model allows unless a role sets `maxTokens`, and one cut off at a limit fails with that reason instead of being patched up.
- **Storyboards cut as fine as asked.** A density per book (sparse, balanced, dense, or left to the director) and a ceiling on panels; panels that may change in the middle of a long line; a director that is asked for the rest when it stops early.
- **The shelf.** Covers on the bookshelf and the overview, any illustration as cover, and removal of a book into the system trash — the book's own files only, nothing erased.
- **Nothing that looks like nothing.** Every button that starts work shows what it is doing until it ends, and a bar at the foot of the Studio lists everything in progress with a way to stop it.
- **Agent surface.** Twenty-two `novel_*` tools covering everything the Studio's screens can do — including its settings (`novel_settings`), showing a screen to the author (`novel_show`) and the small hand edits (`novel_manage`) — a project digest in the prompt, working modes, the `/novel` command, five workflow skills, tool rows that open the Studio, a Studio tab that opens by itself when the showrunner starts working and follows what it shows, and a confirmation gate for writes in read-only sessions, for removing a book and for opening one to the local network.
