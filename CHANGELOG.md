# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Full screen for reading, listening and watching.** The viewer's bar has a way to the whole screen (`F` in and out, `Esc` out). The frame that goes there holds every half of the viewer — the text as page, script or motion comic and film, the people, the world, the side stories — so changing between them stays full. In it the viewer is a reader's: the page, the play bar and the controls of reading; the workshop's own (review notes, the production desk, the way to a phone, to editing, the picture strip over a selection) stay behind and are as they were on leaving. While the pointer rests, the bar and the pointer fade; the strip the bar leaves reads as the page's margin, so nothing jumps. The screen is asked for through the Fullscreen API; where the page may not have it, the frame fills the window instead, and where a host grants it and takes it straight back, likewise. Dialogs opened while full go inside the frame. (`src/client/reader/full.tsx`, among the parts the Studio and the phone reader share.)

- **Which picture of a person leads is the author's to choose.** A design sheet came first, always: drawn from the words alone, with the portrait and everything else drawn against it. The other order is as sound — a portrait the author likes, and a sheet that matches it — so *Settings → Images* has a choice of three in the place of "draw a missing design sheet first" (`image.lead`). *The design sheet* (as before). *The portrait*: a portrait is drawn from the words alone and never against the sheet; the sheet is drawn against the portrait — after it, where there is none ("after it, 1 design sheet drawn against the portrait": asked first, as ever) — told what the reference is for (who they are; not the background, the pose, what is held, the light); other pictures are held against the sheet. *Neither*: nothing is drawn for another picture's sake. References still run one way in each. A sheet or a portrait already there is never redrawn behind the author's back: the page says which has fallen behind. The art batch orders its two steps accordingly; `novel_illustrate kind=sheet` takes `from` (`portrait` | `words`) for one picture. `sheetFirst: false` reads as *neither*.

- **A prompt that fits the model: the director rewrites one that is too long.** An image model reads so many tokens of a prompt and silently drops the rest — and the Studio's prompts end with the art direction and what to keep out. The limit is now a setting (`image.promptTokens`; where unset, what a server of the author's own says of its model, `limits.max_prompt_tokens`), and a prompt that comes to more is written again by the director before the picture is drawn: the same picture in fewer words, in order of what matters, with what to keep out worked in and talk of reference pictures dropped where none go. What is handed in is measured (the Studio's own count, on the safe side, held to nine tenths of the limit) and handed back if over; the nearest try with its end left off, or the prompt as it was, where that fails — and the picture's record says which (`sent.compact`), beside both prompts. A prompt fitted once is kept for the same picture drawn again. Settings → Images: *Prompt limit (tokens)* and a tick box to turn the rewriting off (`image.promptCompact = false`); a listed model shows how many tokens it reads.

- **Pictures from an OpenAI-compatible server of your own.** The custom images endpoint took any address and any model id already, but asked every endpoint as it asks a service on the net: eight pictures at once, at the Studio's own sizes, with a quality where one was chosen, and never without a key. A model on the author's machine or network behind `/v1/images/generations` (`client.images.generate()` in OpenAI's SDK) is now asked the way one graphics card wants to be: **one picture at a time** whatever number is set; each **at the nearest in shape of the sizes it draws** (`image.sizes`; `768x768`, `768x512`, `512x768` while none are named); **sent `model`, `prompt`, `n: 1` and `size` and nothing else** — no quality, no reference pictures; **waited for as long as it says** when it answers `429` with a `Retry-After`; and with **a key only where it wants one**. Which endpoint is such a server is read off its address — this machine, a private network, a Tailscale-like VPN — unless the model is one of OpenAI's, and is said outright with `image.local` where the address misleads; the settings show it as a tick box under the custom endpoint, with the sizes beside it and what each kind of picture comes to. A refusal that carries its words in `detail` (FastAPI's way) is now quoted like any other. Nothing changes for a service on the net: GPT Image is asked as before.

- **Who and what belongs to which line of the plot.** In a world that holds several stories, a character's card and a world entry can name the lines they belong to (`lines`; `main` is the main line) — *Plot lines* on the card and on the entry, shown only in a book with more than one line. One that names none belongs to all; one kept to some lines is left out of what the writer of any other line is told (its card, its entry). Beside a name the page shows the lines it is kept to — or, where nothing is said, the lines the person is seen on, read off the digests along each line.

- **Another story line, imported into the same world.** A book's plot could already hold a line that parts from the story's very beginning; what was missing was a way to bring a finished manuscript in as one. *Import another story line* on the plot graph takes a file (.txt / .md / .epub / .docx), has the editor find its chapters, and puts them on a line of their own that begins where the story begins and joins nothing — sharing the book's bible: people and places it has in common are recognised by name as its chapters are digested, and whoever is new comes up as suggestions. Lives, stages and signature scenes are read along a line as before. To the showrunner: `novel_import action=line` (`line.import`).

- **People grow along the book.** A card said one age and one face, of no chapter in particular. Now it keeps the **stretches of a life** (`stages`): from which chapter on, how old ("about thirteen (a youth)"), what turned, and — where they differ — how the person looks and who they are in that stretch, with a design sheet and a portrait of its own. **The planner finds them**: an agent that reads the book for how old somebody is as they enter and for the points where they become somebody else to look at or to describe (a stage of life, a station, a look) — a stretch is not an episode of the plot, and most people have one to three. It runs as a step of the import analysis (`growth`), for one person from their card (*Growth → Have the planner find the turns*, with a word beside it), and for everyone listed in one go (*Read their growth*). **What follows from a stretch**: the writer is told which one holds at the chapter in hand (age, look, bearing); an illustration or a panel of a chapter shows each person as that chapter has them — the stretch's own anchor and sheet; a portrait of a signature scene shows the stretch that scene falls in. A stretch that looks different gets its own anchor — written by the director from the card's, so that it is the same person in words — and its own sheet, drawn from those words alone (held against the card's sheet, it came back as the card's sheet). To the showrunner: `novel_bible action=derive_growth`, `novel_illustrate … stage=<id>`.

- **Pictures from a model on this machine: ComfyUI and Diffusers.** Two more ways to draw, for trying local models against a book. *ComfyUI* talks to a running server over its HTTP API and runs the author's own workflow (exported in API format, with `%prompt%`, `%negative_prompt%`, `%width%`, `%height%`, `%seed%`, `%steps%`, `%scale%`, `%model%` filled in; a second workflow with `%image1%`… takes the reference pictures) — or a small built-in graph for a plain checkpoint. *Diffusers* starts the author's own Python with a worker that loads the model named (a Hugging Face id, a folder, a `.safetensors` file), keeps it loaded between pictures, and passes each pipeline only what its call takes. Neither holds a key; both keep the picture's record like every service. Settings → Image generation → Service; `image.provider: comfyui | diffusers`.

- **What a review found is mended in batches, across chapters.** Mending issue by issue rewrote one place at a time, and two fixes of the same fact could disagree. The review page now lists every open issue of the book, chapter by chapter, each with a box for what the author says of it — kept with the issue (`note`), not with the screen — and a tick box. *Mend the chosen together* hands the ticked ones, with a word on the batch, to one planner: it reads them side by side, settles which chapters change and how — also chapters nobody named, where they tell of the same facts — and each chapter is rewritten once, for everything that concerns it; the bible's changes wait as suggestions, the chapters' as proposals. *Mend the grave ones* does the same for every open issue from a chosen severity up, in one click. An issue handed over says so until the author settles it. To the showrunner: `novel_revise scope=issues` (`issues` as `<chapter>:<issue id>`, or `severity`). The same tick boxes and the same bar are in the editor's review panel and in the reader's list of a chapter's notes; on the review page the issues are read chapter by chapter — a row per chapter that unfolds its own. **Once proposals are accepted, what is still open is looked at again**: the reviewer reads the open issues of the chapters that changed — and those handed over with the same batch, wherever they are — against the text as it stands; what the change settled is marked settled (with why), what reads differently has its quote and wording brought up to the text, and the Studio says how many of each. **What was made to mend an issue shows beneath that issue**: the diff, with *Accept* and *Reject*, right where the fix was asked for — on the review page, in the editor's panel, in the reader — instead of a trip to the proposals tab (a proposal carries the issues it mends, `issues`; a rewrite that mends several of a chapter's is folded under each). *Check again* on the review page asks for the same look at any time (`review.recheck`); `recheckIssues: false` in the settings turns the automatic one off.

- **Signature scenes: the moments a person is remembered by, and a portrait of any of them.** A portrait used to be a face on a plain ground, the same picture for everybody. Now a character's card keeps their signature scenes — Lin Daiyu burying the flowers, first — in a panel of its own (*Signature scenes*, `visual.scenes`): what each is called, the chapter it is from (a click reads it), the moment written as a picture, why it is theirs, and the portrait drawn of it. The director finds them in the book — an agent that reads the text, handed the digests' notes on the person as leads — most their own first, as many as there are; the author rewrites them, adds and removes them, marks which one a portrait shows by default, and has the list looked over again with a wish beside it. Beside the portrait button a choice says which moment the next portrait shows (or a plain likeness), and each row draws its own. Nobody is stopped to be asked before a first portrait: the list is there to read and to choose from. **What the author asks of one portrait guides the pick instead of replacing it**: "brighter" has the director settle — among the moments kept, or another of the book's — the one that answers to it, and say how the wish was used; that moment is for the one picture, and one click adds it to the list. **Which moments are fit to be shown is the author's to say** — the book's avoid list, what they ask: the task rules no kind of moment out (a martyr's portrait may be the martyrdom, a general's the fight), with one line that is not the author's to move — somebody under age is not written into a moment of intimacy or nudity. A book with nothing written is not asked, and somebody the book has no moment for gets the plain portrait and the reason. The interlude stage draws plain figures; `image.portraitScene = false` turns it off. A card of an earlier version that kept one paragraph (`visual.scene`) reads as a list of one. To the showrunner: `novel_bible action=derive_scene` (`direction` guides it), `novel_illustrate kind=portrait scene=<id>`, `prompt` / `notes` on a portrait guide its moment, `plain=true` draws the plain one.

- **How a picture came to be, beside the picture.** Opening a picture large — somebody's portrait or design sheet on their card, the design art of a place or a thing, anything in the gallery — shows all there is on record of its making: how it stands against the book as it is now (is it the picture in use; were the visual anchor and the art direction changed since; was a portrait drawn against the sheet in use, an earlier one, or none); the reference pictures that went with the prompt, each opening its own record, so that a look can be followed back from picture to picture; what the prompt was put together from, part by part in the order sent, with where each part is changed; and what the image model was sent, word for word. For that, a picture now keeps the parts of its subject (`parts.pieces`), what its prompt said of the references (`parts.referenceNote`), the service and the size it was drawn with, and `sent` — the prompt as it left, with what the way to that model adds: the things to keep out where the service has no field for them, the shape of the frame where it takes no size, an agent's wrapping. The showrunner reads the same with `novel_manage action=show_image`. It is the author's alone: the reader on a phone and a published site carry none of it.

### Fixed

- **A prompt no longer speaks of reference pictures that are not sent.** A way to the model that takes none — a server of the author's own, any model but GPT Image on an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, ComfyUI with no workflow for them, a model OpenRouter lists without `input_references` — got "Reference images: #1 shows …; whoever they show must match their reference" all the same, with no picture behind it, and the record of the picture warned that the prompt spoke of references and none were sent. Every such way now says so before anything is drawn (`PromptRoom.references`): nothing is read off the disk for it, the prompt is written without a word of any, and what stayed behind is kept with the picture (`parts.left`) and said in plain words beside it. The question before a picture ("this will first draw N design sheets") says where the sheets would not go along. Where it cannot be known beforehand (Diffusers, until the pipeline is loaded), the warning stays, in clearer words.

- **A picture of the author's own prompt knows the people it names.** *New picture → Free prompt* lets the author tick who is in it, and said their visual anchors and design sheets went along — only the sheets did. A service that takes reference pictures knew them by those; a server of the author's own, which takes none, had "she" and drew anybody (the same line twice: a white robe, then a black one). Each named person is in the words now, after what was asked: their name and how they look — the anchor, or the card's prose where there is none, with the outfit chosen for the picture. Nobody is added to a prompt sent as written (`raw`).

- **What is picked looks picked.** A handful of rules coloured a chosen thing with `--nv-accent`, a variable the stylesheet never defined — so the chosen lines of the plot on a card, the first of somebody's signature scenes, and the focus ring of the album's line choice showed nothing, and the new tick marks of a batch were transparent over a portrait. They take the brand colour now.

- **A server of the author's own is asked what its model draws, and a picture drawn again is another picture.** Run against the real server for the first time (a FastAPI app in front of Qwen-Image 2.1, plain and Turbo), three things were not as assumed. *Sizes*: it takes any side from 256 to 768 in steps of 32, not three sizes — and says how large a model draws in its model list (`GET /models`: `limits`), so a 16:9 scene is now asked for at 736×416 instead of the 3:2 of the nearest of three. *Seed*: it draws from seed 42 unless sent another, so the same prompt gave the very same picture, pixel for pixel — every request now carries a seed of its own where the list shows a default one, kept in the picture's record (`sent.seed`). *Prompt*: it refuses one longer than its `max_prompt_chars` (2000) — the prompt is cut to that. The settings show beside a model what the server says of it, and what each kind of picture comes to; the author's own list of sizes still has the last word, and a server that lists nothing is asked as before.

- **OpenRouter's image models are asked for what each of them takes.** Every picture went out with a shape, a fineness (`resolution`) and `output_format: png`, "which every model there understands" — and they do not: 36 of the 59 models behind `/images`, GPT Image among them, have no fineness to choose, the others have different tiers (Gemini has neither `768` nor `1.5K`, which is what a design sheet was asked at), and a model refuses a value it does not have (*resolution: not supported. Accepted: 512, 1K, 2K, 4K*). With the sheet drawn first, every picture of a person failed on such a model. The Studio now reads OpenRouter's own listing of what each image model takes (`GET /images/models`) and sends those parameters only, each at the nearest value the model has; a quality the model does not know and reference pictures beyond what it holds stay behind. What the providers an account may use refuse on top of that is read from the refusal (*Accepted: …*) and asked for next, and remembered. An account whose privacy settings leave a model no provider is told so, with where the setting is. The model list offered in the settings is the images endpoint's own.

### Changed

- **The bible, line by line.** Every page of the bible listed all of it whichever line was in hand, and whatever a digest found on a line beside the main one became the whole book's — handed to the main line's writer too. Now: **the digest says whose each new thing is** — in a book of several lines it is told which story the chapter is of and answers, in the same pass, whether a thing is that story's alone or the world's (`newEntities[].scope`); its suggestion says so, and a thing of the story alone is filed, when taken up, under the lines that read the chapter (where a digest did not say: a thing found beside the main line is that line's, one found on the main line the world's). A book of one line is asked nothing of the kind. **The bible's pages list the line in hand** (places, houses, things, the world): the line's own entries above, what the lines share in a group of its own below, with a choice at their head — each line and how many it has, *filed under none*, *every line*; along a line beside the main one, how often an entry is named and how much it weighs are read along that line (`world.presence`). An entry nobody filed stays every line's — it is not guessed from where the text happens to spell its name out, since an entry a whole book stands on may be named on one line only — but where the text names it is said beside it (`lines.seen` → `world`).

- **A batch filed under lines of the plot at once.** *Select* over the bible's lists and the list of people turns a click on a row into a tick; *File under lines…* then says which lines the ticked ones belong to — those and no other — or takes the filing back. *File by where the text has them* does it by the book: each under the lines it is found on, an entry where the text names it and a person where the chapters have them; what every line has, or none yet, is left as it was and counted. (`lines.assign`, with `byText`; to the showrunner `novel_bible action=file`.)

- **More image models recommended on OpenRouter (issue 2 of the recommendations).** Beside GPT Image 2.5, which stays the default, the model field now offers the models that were drawn with for real through the Studio on 2026-10-09 — a design sheet and a portrait against it on each: Gemini 3 Pro Image, Gemini 3.1 Flash Image, Grok Imagine Image 2.0 and Grok Imagine Image Quality — ahead of GPT Image 2.5 Flare, Seedream 5.0 Pro, GPT Image 2 and Qwen-Image 3 Pro. No setting moves: the recommended model itself is what it was.

- **Mendings no longer lock each other out: in line, or apart.** While one mending ran, the buttons that start another said "mending…" and waited. Now any number can be asked for, and a small triangle beside the buttons says how a new one is taken (`order` on `ai.fixIssue`, `ai.fixIssues`, `ai.reviseBook`; on the showrunner's `novel_revise`): **in line** (the default — the surer way) waits for the mendings asked for before it and then goes on from what they left waiting; **apart** starts at once on the text as it stands, for mendings that have nothing to do with each other — another line of the plot, a far part of the story. Going on from what waits (`onto`): the writer reads a chapter as it would stand with its waiting changes accepted. A passage the book still has as it was gets a proposal of its own, as before; a passage a waiting change made, or a chapter rewritten whole, gets **one** proposal for the chapter with all of it in, which stands for the ones it took in (`supersedes`, `batches`; they are settled by it, `supersededBy`) and carries their issues — accepted, they are all in; turned down, they come back to wait by themselves. A change applied outright never goes on from what waits: it accepts nothing on the author's behalf. While mendings run, the bar says how many and what a new one will do.
- **A graph built for a ComfyUI model is sampled as that server samples it.** The built-in graph used guidance 7 whatever the model — right for a Stable Diffusion checkpoint, and harsh on a newer diffusion model (a picture drawn at 7 where the model's own workflows use 3 came out hard-edged and flat). Where the settings leave steps or guidance empty, the Studio now reads the server's history for its latest run that loaded this model and made a picture — the author's own runs before the Studio's — and takes its steps, guidance, sampler and scheduler; with no such run, 7 for a checkpoint and 3.5 for a diffusion model that comes alone. What a picture was sampled with, and where the figures came from, is kept with it (`sent.sampling`) and shown in its record. Workflows are run as they are, as before.
- **The Outline's chapters are listed one line of the plot at a time, and deriving is part of that page.** Every line's chapters stood one under the other in a single column, and what comes next was asked of the planner on a tab of its own, which did not know which line was in hand. The chapters page now has the line chosen at its head — the main one with its volumes, or another as it is read: what it shares with the line it parts from, greyed, then its own — and *Derive* on the same bar opens the planner's panel beside the list, for the line shown. A chapter opened from elsewhere takes the page to its line. The Derive tab is gone.
- **One line of the plot in hand, on every page.** Each page kept its own choice of line — the outline, the people, the writing, the reading, the album — and the production board had none: somebody at work on a line beside the main one chose it again on every page they came to. The Studio now has one line in hand (`snapshot.line`, kept per book on the device): what the outline lists, what is derived, the chapters of the write and read pages, the album, whose people are listed, the production board and a book-wide revision are all that line's. Changed on any page, it is changed on all; a chapter opened from elsewhere that the line does not read takes the Studio to its own line, and a line taken in hand puts the Studio on a chapter of it. *Produce in one go* got its selector, and lists a line's own chapters (what it shares with the line it parts from is produced on that line).
- **A release no longer takes an open Studio off the screen.** The host swaps the Studio's code in place and renders the open page with the new code on the old page's state; where a page's hooks differ between the two versions that render throws, and the host removed the whole Studio. The Studio now catches a render error at its root and puts its pages up afresh — same book, same page — and hands the error on only when it comes straight back.
- **A run of chapters is written in order, or side by side.** A batch always wrote one chapter after the other, each waiting for the one before and its digest — the way to chapters that agree, and slow. The start button of *Batch: writing and upkeep* now has a small triangle beside it: *In order* (as before, and the default) or *Side by side* — as many chapters at once as the settings allow, their digests side by side too, the arcs brought up once at the end. Written side by side, a chapter is given its own outline, the text there was before the batch, and — new in the writer's context — the outlines of the chapters before it that are not written yet, with the word that their text is not there and their part is not to be told again. The choice is kept. `novel_batch what=text order=together` for the showrunner, which keeps to the order unless the author asks for speed.
- **ComfyUI: a workflow saved on the server is chosen from a list and used as it is.** The settings wanted a file on this computer, exported in API format, with placeholders written into it by hand — while the workflow sits on the machine ComfyUI runs on, saved by its editor in a format its own server does not run. *Settings → Images → Workflows saved on the server* now lists them; choosing one fetches it, turns it into the graph the server runs (what each node takes is asked of the server; notes, a seed's "after generate", the upload button and what leads to no output are left out), and writes the placeholders: words, size, seed, and each `LoadImage` as a reference picture's place. One that loads pictures becomes the workflow for pictures with references, any other the plain one; each is kept in the settings with where it came from. With that, pictures drawn on ComfyUI hold to a design sheet: drawn for real with a one-reference workflow, the same person in another scene.
- **ComfyUI: what the server has is asked of the server.** *Settings → Images → Connect* lists the server's checkpoints, diffusion models, text encoders and VAEs, with its version and device, and the model is chosen from the list instead of typed. A server that holds its models as parts — a diffusion model beside a text encoder and a VAE, and no checkpoint — used to answer "no checkpoint to draw with"; the built-in graph now loads such a model with `UNETLoader`, `CLIPLoader` and `VAELoader` (`image.textEncoder`, `image.vae`, `image.encoderType`, each chosen from the server's own lists), and where a choice is left open the error names it. *Use it as the workflow* takes the server's last run that made a picture as the template, kept in the settings: the words, the size and the seed become the Studio's to fill, the models and the sampling stay as the server ran them. Drawn for real for the first time, on a ComfyUI 0.39 server, both ways.

- **A digest proposes nobody for a card; the planner says who is worth one.** Every name a chapter's digest met and the bible did not know became a "new character" suggestion — the doorman as much as the rival — and a long book buried its cast under a hundred of them. A digest no longer does that. Every name without a card is on the list of walk-ons, as it always was; once digests have settled, the planner looks through the names it has not looked at yet, side by side and with the book to read (`siftCast`, the job *sift cast*), and says of each: **worth a card** — a suggestion marked as the planner's, with its reason, a part and a line of who they are —, **another name** of somebody — a suggestion of a new kind (`alias`) that folds the name into that card when accepted, or, where it is another name of somebody found worth a card in the same pass, an alias on that suggestion —, or a **walk-on**, left on the list with what the planner said of them. The judgement is the planner's: no threshold of appearances. A name looked at is not asked about again until it is seen in more chapters. Nothing is made or folded by the pass. The suggestions an older version made chapter by chapter are looked through with the rest: kept with a reason, turned into another name, or taken back. People → Walk-ons shows what the planner said beside each name and has *Have the planner look (N)*; Review → Suggestions lists the planner's first, says how many names were left as walk-ons, and offers the pass while anything is unlooked-at. *Settings → After digests, have the planner look through the new names* (`siftCast`) switches the automatic pass off. For the showrunner: `novel_manage action=sift_walkons`.

- **The interface says what a thing is, and stops.** Hints, tooltips, confirmations and notices all over the Studio and the reader explained how things work behind them, repeated what the button beside them already said, or added an aside in brackets. They were gone through one by one — some four hundred of them — and cut to what the author needs at that spot: a hint is one short sentence or none, a tooltip a phrase, a confirmation what will happen and whether it can be undone. The English text was cut with the Chinese, and calls the host DSH throughout.

- **A look is written with the art direction in hand, and the period it fixes holds over the source text.** An art direction may say more than how to paint: "dress and buildings of the Ming". Appended after the subject of a picture it could only colour what the subject had already said — and the director, who writes a character's visual anchor from the card and the text, never saw it: an anchor followed the novel's words to "one thick queue", and no art direction could take the queue out again. The director is now handed the art direction wherever it writes down a look that lasts — a visual anchor, ideas sorted into one, the look of a place or a thing, a place at a turn of its course — and told what of it binds: the forms of a period (how hair is dressed, the cut of clothes, how a house is built) are written as the art direction fixes them, not carried over from the text and not blended with it; what is the person's or the place's own is kept; the forms most likely to creep in go onto the never-drawn list. Scenes, covers and storyboards are told the same of what they describe. An anchor written again keeps what nothing speaks against (*Have the director rewrite* on the card). Anchors already written do not change by themselves: *Bible → Art* says so and lists whose looks are written down, each a step from where it is rewritten.

- **A card no longer says "active" or "dead".** A character's card carried one word for where they stood in the book, beside the life read off the digests; the two were told apart, and the word was still there — on the card's head, in its form, on a board of five columns. It is gone. A book is not one straight road: it has lines of the plot that part and chapters written again after the ones that follow them, and a word on a card is true of one line at one moment. Where somebody is, is their **life on the page**, along the line looked at: under their name in a line ("comes on in chapter 9 · dies in chapter 12"), chapter by chapter below it, and in a paragraph that is theirs. The lifecycle board and the presence grid are one table now — *People → Lives*: a row a person, a column a chapter of the line; on the page, coming on or back, leaving, dying, planned by the outline, planned but missing, point of view. In the lists a death or a leaving stands with its chapter, and nothing stands for everybody else. A `status:` line in the front matter of an older card is not read, and is left out when the card is next saved; digests no longer ask "mark as dead?" — the digest says it by itself.
- **The writer is told who is gone as of the chapter in hand, on that chapter's own line.** The list of the dead came from the labels: whoever was marked dead was dead to the writer of every chapter, the fifth written again as much as the fortieth, and on every line. It is read off the digests of the chapters *before* the one in hand now, along its line — with those who left for good beside the dead, each with their chapter. Everyone's card travels the same whatever the chapter, with no word on it that changes as the story moves: the head of a request stays what a provider has cached. An interlude offers who is alive where it is set, not who is alive at the end.
- **The sheet comes first.** A portrait asked for somebody without a design sheet draws the sheet first, and the portrait against it; a scene, a panel and a cover do the same for whoever of their people comes back in the book (a part that carries it, or in two chapters or more). It is never done behind the author's back: the job says "first the design sheet of …" while it runs, the picture names the sheets drawn for it when it is done (to the showrunner as well), a batch of portraits shows the sheets as steps of their own, and two pictures that need one sheet at once wait for one drawing of it. `image.sheetFirst = false` turns it off.
- **The notes on the ensemble can be put away.** Above the People page they were a block on every tab but one, and a line that could not be dismissed on that one. They are one line by default now, on every tab; they unfold, fold, and *Hide* takes them out of the way — a small mark at the end of the tabs, with their number, brings them back. What they say no longer speaks of labels: who with a part has been away, whom the outline casts after their death, how many are on the page at once, which lead lacks a motive or an arc.
- **Before sheets are drawn first, the author is asked.** Wherever a picture is started — a portrait, a passage, a chapter's panels, the cover, the gallery, a batch, one-click production — the Studio works out whose design sheets it would draw first (`image.sheetsFirst`, nothing is drawn) and, where there are any, says "this will first draw N design sheets: …" with three answers: draw them first, not this time, cancel. Where nobody lacks a sheet nothing is asked. The showrunner asks the same way (`sheet_first=ask | draw | skip` on `novel_illustrate` and `novel_comic`).
- **A mark of the author's own in a life.** A digest misses things, gets things wrong, and cannot see what lies before the first page. *Write a mark* on a person's page puts down that they come on, come back, leave or die at a chapter — or were dead or gone before the story begins. It is an event with its chapter, not a state: it holds over the digest of that chapter and on every line that reads it; after a death or a leaving written down this way, later chapters only remember the person until a return is written. Marks show as the author's on the strip, in the table of lives and in the notes; they are kept on the card (`lifeMarks`), and the showrunner writes them with `novel_bible action=mark_life`. The writer is told of "dead before the story begins" like of any other death.
- **Ideas jotted down beside the buttons that draw somebody.** A box under *Draw portrait* and *Design sheet* takes whatever comes to mind, mixed as it comes. The director sorts it when the picture is drawn: what is of the person goes into their look for good — the visual anchor, and a sentence of the appearance where the card lacks it —, what is of this one picture ("a snowy night, looking back") directs this picture and is kept nowhere, and what the picture has no place for is left out. How each idea was sorted is listed when the picture is done; where no model answers nothing is guessed. To the showrunner it is `notes` on `novel_illustrate`.

### Fixed

- **A line of the plot is worked on from that line alone.** A book may hold several stories in one world, each from its own first page; whoever worked on one was still handed the others. The tools every role reads the book with knew the main line only: a planner deriving chapters for another line that looked at the outline saw the main line's, "chapter 3" read the main line's third chapter, the cast and the threads were the whole book's, and a search found passages of any line — so chapters derived for a side story leaned on another story, numbered by it. Now a piece of work says which line it is on, and its tools are kept to it (`core/reading.ts`, `bookTools(…, line)`): the outline, the chapter numbers, memories, reviews, the cast with each life read along the line, the threads as they stand at its end, and what a search finds. The context a chapter is written, derived or reviewed with keeps to the line the same way (`Project.lineScope`): **people** are a line's where the author said so, else where they are — in its chapters by their digests or cast by their plans; a card no chapter has yet is every line's — so nobody has to be marked by hand; **threads** are the lines' that planted or advanced them, and one paid off on the main line still waits on a line that parted before; the book's **premise** — the main line's story — gives way to the line's own name and what the author said of it where the line is apart from the first page; and the planner's overview no longer lists "the other ways the story goes". Somebody named by a chapter's plan or by the task itself is still given, whichever line they are of. The showrunner is kept to no line: `novel_read` and `novel_search` take `line`, the whole outline lists every line with its own numbering, and `novel_crew` takes the line a task is on.
- **Deriving from the Outline went to the main line whatever line was in hand.** The Derive tab started on the main line; with a chapter of another line selected it now starts on that line.
- **Review issues of other lines were dropped when mended in a batch, and their chapters were not on the review page.** Batch mending and book-wide revision looked at the main line only: an issue found in a chapter of another line was silently left out, and "mend the grave ones" never saw it. A batch is now mended line by line — one planner a line, reading that line — and a book-wide revision can be asked of a line. The review page lists every line's chapters under the line's name, each with the number it has there.
- **Choosing a line on the People page changes who is listed.** The choice only changed along which line lives were read. It now lists that line's people (with how many each line has), says how many more belong to other lines only, and offers "every line".
- **A character's growth and signature scenes are read along the line that character is of**, not along the main line whoever they are.

- **A design sheet drawn again came back as the sheet before.** A character's main sheet was drawn with their current sheet — lacking one, their portrait — as a reference image, under a line that told the image model the hair and the clothing must match it. So nothing the author changed could reach it: an anchor rewritten, an art direction given a period ("dress and buildings of the Ming"), and the model handed back the old sheet with a new background. The main sheet is drawn from the words alone now — the visual anchor, the art direction, what is asked of this one — every time, also where somebody has a portrait and no sheet; it is what every other picture of them is held against, and a picture cannot be held against itself. A look (outfit) is still drawn against the main sheet, and its prompt now says what that reference is for — the face, hair colour and build from the picture, the outfit from the words — instead of also saying that the clothing must match. (The Codex CLI was told the same "keep hair and clothing faithful" around every prompt with references; it is told to keep what the description says to keep.) A portrait drawn before the sheet in use says so on the card, until it is drawn again.
- **Opening the motion comic of a chapter took the whole Studio off the screen.** The page that asks before sheets are drawn was given its hook below the place where the page returns early while a chapter's script and storyboard load; once they had arrived the page rendered with one hook more than before, React threw, and the host removed the Studio — a blank page where a book was. The hook is asked for before the returns. Nothing of this shows in a type check or a build, so the sources are read for it now: `tests/hooks.test.ts` goes through every screen of the Studio and the reader for a hook reached after an early return.
- **A book kept inside another book's folder: the Studio beside an agent opened the outer one.** Which book a conversation is on was looked for in its folder before anything else but the author's own choice. A role's run is a new conversation, handed the folder of the conversation it works for — and a folder can be a book of its own with others inside it (the first book made in an empty workspace is the folder; the next go inside). So the Studio brought beside a scribe recording a chapter of the inner book showed the outer book's overview, and a subagent the showrunner sent out was told the outer book was in play. A conversation that works for another one is now on the book that work is about — the one its task names, else the book of the conversation it works for — and only then on the book of its folder. The Studio beside it stays on the book, and the page, the author was on.
- **Stepping from the Studio into an agent at work took the Studio away.** A role's run is a conversation of its own, with a column on the right that has nothing in it. Pressing a progress bar (*Watch the planner*), a run in the list of jobs or a cell of a batch's table now takes the Studio along: it is put beside the run, on the book and the page the author was on — from the Studio's own page as well as from a Studio beside a conversation, every time it is asked, also where the author had closed it there before. Two things had been wrong. The Studio's own page was never followed at all (only a Studio beside a conversation was). And DSH drops a page that is opened in a conversation's column in the moment the conversation is first shown — so beside an agent that had only just started, the very case of watching one at work, nothing appeared; the page is now opened once the column is set up, and again for as long as no Studio stands there (`src/client/beside.ts`).
- **A chapter could start playing by itself two chapters after one ran out.** A chapter that ran out goes on to the next, which starts once its script has arrived. Where the next had no script, the wish was kept — and the first chapter with a script that the reader opened afterwards started by itself. The wish is for the chapter gone on to and no other.
- **A picture deleted from the gallery left whoever held it pointing at a file that was gone.** A portrait, a design sheet, a look's sheet, the design art of a place or a thing: the card or the entry went on naming the deleted file and showed a broken picture. Deleting a picture now takes it off whoever held it up as theirs and says whose it was (in the gallery, and to the showrunner); where chapter text shows it, the text is not touched, as before.
- **On a low, wide screen the motion comic was squeezed to a strip.** Its picture stood in a column that scrolls and gave up its height to it; and where it kept its height it was cut to the window's shape instead of keeping its own. It keeps both now.
- **A button that waited for a job could go on saying it was busy.** The list of running jobs is brought up to date by the Studio's watch, which rests while the Studio is out of sight; whoever waited for a job to end now brings the list up to date themselves when it does.
- **When a site was last published was shown by the clock of Greenwich**, not the computer's.
- **Chapters of a line beside the main one were never digested on request.** *Digest* went through the main line's chapters only: a chapter of another line that was named was passed over without a word, and none of them counted as pending. Every line's chapters are digested now — which is what lives, the world along a line and the comparison of lines are read from.
- **A published site lost every picture and sound whose file had a name outside ASCII.** Media were written under percent-escaped names, and a host decodes an address before it looks for the file — so a portrait named after its character in Chinese was never found. Files are written under their own names now; publishing into the same folder again puts them right and removes the wrongly named ones.
- **The cover on a book's overview spread over the page.** Once the cover became the way into reading it sat inside a button, where the rule that sizes it no longer reached it: it was shown at the size it was painted, over the title and the premise.
- **A chapter's video had no subtitles to see.** Post-production put them into the file as a subtitle track where this computer's ffmpeg could not draw text (Homebrew's cannot), and the Studio's player — a web player — shows nothing of a track inside an MP4. The Studio now keeps the track's words beside the file and its player shows them; a video cut before this gets them the first time it is opened. The line under the player says whether the words are in the picture or carried as a track.
- **A chapter's page in the outline listed everyone in the book.** The plan editor laid out every character and every thread of the book to be ticked — 345 names and 146 threads in an imported *Dream of the Red Chamber* — which read as a chapter with everybody in it. It now shows who is in the chapter and which threads it touches; what the digest found beyond the plan is offered to add; anyone else comes through a picker that narrows by the weight of the part and by name.
- **A subtitle that was nothing but a closing quote.** Lines are cut into pieces a screen holds; a closing quote, a mark of an ellipsis, now stay with what they close, an opening quote goes on with what it opens, and joined paragraphs leave no gap in a script that has no spaces.
- **A chapter could be removed, by whoever found out how.** The way to do it showed only under the pointer. It is always there now, on every row of both chapter lists; several chapters can be ticked and removed together.
- **Stepping into an agent's conversation took the Studio away.** DSH keeps a column of pages per conversation, and a role's run is a conversation of its own with nothing in its column. Where the conversation the author comes from had the Studio beside it and the one they step into is about the same book, the Studio opens there too, on the same page.
- **A cut that ran out of tool calls had nothing to hand in.** The note of how much of its budget a role has used rode on the role's own tools only, and post-production works in a shell: it never read one. The note now comes beside whatever tool was used last (DSH's `tools/post-execute`), at half, three quarters and nine tenths, and a task may add what it still owes — for a cut, "nothing has passed the checks yet". What an unfinished cut left in its working directory is named to the next one. Post-production's default budget is 200 calls, up from 80.
- **A chapter added after one was removed could get its number** — and with it the digest, versions and voices of the removed one.
- Where ffmpeg is missing, the hint how to install it named macOS only.

- **The Studio no longer falls out from under an author whose DSH runs off a checkout.** A build used to empty `lib/` and write it again; a DSH that was running swapped the Studio in its window for whatever it found there — and dropped back to the conversation — while its Host half ran on as it had been loaded. Builds now go to a directory of their own and are put into `lib/` file by file, each one whole, only the files that changed, the entry DSH watches last (`npm run release`); day-to-day builds do not touch `lib/` at all (`npm run check` builds a staged copy the dev launcher runs).
- **A Studio that is newer than its Host says so.** When the plugin is updated on disk while DSH runs, DSH puts the new Studio in the window at once and keeps the Host half it started with. The Studio now knows — the Host names the build it runs and the one installed — and says "restart DSH" at its top instead of failing with `unknown method`; a call an older Host does not know says the same.
- **Swapped or reloaded, the Studio is where it was**: the same book, view, chapter and tab, and its own page is open again if it was.
- **The leads of an imported book were missing from its cast.** Two causes. What the chapter digests found was kept in a list of two hundred, and the oldest were dropped first — in an imported book, the people met in the first chapters. And an import that was cut short never came to make the cast at all. Nothing found is dropped now; see *The cast of an imported book* below.
- A conversation started in a folder that is itself a locked book showed "could not reach the Novel Studio service" beside it; it shows the lock. The showrunner is told which other books are in reach, and a conversation can be moved from a locked book onto another.
- Conversations of a sealed book that were left in the open when DSH quit are taken under its seal when DSH starts — the pass ran before DSH's session store was up and found nothing. After unlocking or locking in the Studio, DSH's list of conversations is read again, so what came back shows at once.
- Reading a side line of the plot, the chapter where it left the main line did not offer the main line as a way on.

Digesting a chapter now fills what its plan leaves empty (a line of synopsis, place, time, cast, threads) from what the chapter turned out to be; nothing that is written is replaced. *Fill from the digests* on the outline page does it for a book digested before this.

### Changed

- **What plays in a chapter, and how the page behaves while it does, is said once** (`src/client/reader/playback.ts`): the Studio's viewer and the reader on other devices each had their own copy of the player's wiring — turning to the script when play is pressed over the prose, keeping the line being spoken in sight, starting the chapter after one that ran out. Both use `useChapterPlayback` now, and the *Watch* tab is the mode switch's own. Nothing a reader sees has changed.
- **Places, houses, things and ideas each have a page.** The bible's *World* tab listed everything that is not a person in one list. It is four pages now: **Places**, **Houses** (families and organizations), **Things**, and **World** — which keeps the ideas: rules, premises, institutions, history. They are still one kind of entry with a category; changing an entry's category moves it to its page.
- **What is somewhere or something has a weight in the story**: core, major, minor, or passed once — read off how many chapters name it (by its name or one of its other names, or where a chapter's digest wrote the place down), unless the author sets it. Places, houses and things are listed by weight, heaviest first, those passed once folded away; an entry's page says in which chapters it is named. This is not the entry's *importance*, which decides whether its text travels with every request to a model and is now labelled as that. Place art in a batch defaults to the places of weight; a page says which core and major places and things still have no picture.
- **What a reader is given is made in one place.** The small server for other devices assembled the books, chapters, scripts and storyboards it serves by itself. That is one module of the engine now (`core/reader.ts`), which the server asks and a published site is written from — so what a phone shows and what a site shows cannot drift apart.
- **The reader has three faces and one play button.** *Read* and *Listen* were two modes, and the motion comic played on another screen. A chapter now has three faces in the reader — **Prose**, **Script** (who says what, how, over which sound, a line each) and **Watch** (the motion comic, where the chapter has one) — and listening is no longer a mode but the **player** under the first two: press play on either, the line being spoken is marked where it stands by a tint that runs through it at the pace of the voice, and the page follows. Pressing play over the prose turns to the script, unless *Turn to the script on play* is switched off. The script shows the chapter's pictures where the prose has them. The shelf's *Watch* leads here.
- **The reader on a phone, and on a published site, is the same reader.** It has the three faces and the one player too: *Prose*, *Script*, *Watch*, the line being spoken marked at the pace of the voice, pressing play turning to the script. The script as a reader has it — speaker, line, the sound under it, the chapter's pictures where the prose has them — is one page the Studio and the phone share; the Studio's table of takes, with its tools, is what *Produce* brings.
- **Reading settings are one panel.** Type size, paper, typeface and the speed of the voice in sight; behind *More*, whether play turns to the script, whether a chapter goes on to the next, the sound notes beside the text, the sound under the voice (and, in the Studio, the notes of a review). It opens from the reader's bar and from the player; on a phone it comes up from the bottom. *Settings → Reading* in the Studio is the same panel, not a second form. What is set is kept on the device it was set on.
- **Listening is for listening.** The production desk of the audio version, and the tools on every line of the script, used to take the top of the page whenever a chapter was listened to. They come with the *Produce* button in the reader's bar now, and go with it.
- **The home page is the shelf.** With no book open the Studio used to show a short shelf above a long form for making a book. The shelf is the page now. Above it, a banner with the two things an author comes for otherwise: **Talk to the showrunner** — which asks which book the conversation is about, opens (or makes) that book's own conversation, and sends *a new novel* to where books are made — and **New work**, which has a page of its own for starting a book from an idea or bringing one in whole. Every book on the shelf has the ways into it under its cover — *Read*, *Listen*, *Watch*, each shown only where the book has something to read, hear or watch, each opening at the first chapter that does — and *Work on it* on the cover's corner. At the foot: who made this, under what licence, the project's site and the author's page. The whale maid of the icon is on the banner, on the empty shelf and at the head of *New work*.
- **One page for a book's people.** Characters had two homes — their cards under *Bible*, and the lifecycle board, presence grid and relationship graph under *Cast* — each with a list of its own. They are one page now, **People** (人物), beside *Bible* in the bar: *Cards*, *Lifecycle*, *Presence*, *Relationships*, *Walk-ons*, with one filter above them all saying who the page is about. *Bible* keeps the world, the prose style and the art direction. Every way to "open the card" — the board, the graph, a search hit, a tool row in the conversation — leads to the card on that page.
- **A person's page shows the person.** The portrait was a thumbnail beside the name, and the design sheet lay far down the form. The picture now stands large in a column of its own that stays in view while the form scrolls: the portrait, with the design sheet and a sheet per look as thumbnails to switch to, each opening at full size; drawing and redrawing are right under it. Beside it the name, the part and the stage of life, the one-line identity and where they have been on the page — then the form. Narrow, the picture goes above.
- **The settings page starts short.** *Models* is two rows — the writer's model, and one model for the whole crew — and pictures, speech, sound and retrieval are one line each that says what is in use and unfolds when pressed. A switch, *Advanced*, brings the rest and stays as it was left: a block per role with its model, reasoning level, temperature and output cap and — what used to be a second section, *The crew* — whether it works as an agent, its tools and its steps; how many requests go out at once; the context budgets, now with fields for `context.sideTokens` and `context.playTokens`; the shape of each kind of picture and `image.references`. A link that leads to a part of the page opens it, whatever was folded.
- **Nothing in the plugin is compiled, and nothing in it belongs to one system.** The helper that asked a Mac's owner to prove presence before a sealed book's key is released was a small Swift program built with the plugin. It is gone, with its build step: the Mac is asked through `osascript`, as Windows is through PowerShell, and a book opens with its password where a system offers no such check. DSH itself has no owner check a plugin could call (see `docs/design.md`); when it has, the Studio will use it.
- **Work an agent can do is not done for it.** The Studio gives material, says what is wanted and checks the result; commands are the agent's. And the limit that binds the plugin — to run wherever DSH runs — does not bind an agent at work, which faces one computer and may use whatever that computer has.

- **Limits nobody chose are gone.** What a section of the context holds is decided by its share of the model's window and nothing else: the one-line list of other characters (it stopped at 80), open threads (30), relevant lore (8), retrieved passages (16). The import no longer stops at 90 people and 140 world names, or tells the planner "40 at most"; the world, like the cast, is collected in rounds. A derivation is as long as it is asked for, previewed or not. Every picture of a chapter is material for its video. All of an author's style samples are read as far as nine thousand characters go. The book's record keeps five thousand entries (it kept three hundred).
- **The ensemble's notes weigh the part.** Somebody with a part who has gone missing is a note; small parts that are not seen again are one line together, with one button to mark them offstage. "Too many active at once" counts those with a part.
- The agent half (`lib/agent.js`) holds no code of its own any more: it asks the Host half that is running to mount the tools, so the two a session works with are always of one build.

### Changed

- **How much goes out at once is one number per kind of request, and it holds over everything.** Lines spoken, pictures drawn, sounds made and pieces of text work each pass through a gate that counts across every chapter and every job. The default is 8 and the most is 256 (it was 3, at most 8, for speech; 2 for pictures and sounds; 2–4 for text). When a service answers "too fast", the gate slows down by itself — fewer at once, a pause — makes the refused request again instead of losing it, and climbs back as requests go through; the Studio says so while it lasts. Speech figures that were only ever an old default (2 or 3) follow the new one.
- **The story so far travels as an outline and as text.** A model that works on a chapter reads the nearest chapters word for word — whole chapters, as many as half of its context window holds, or the author's figure — and the chapters before those as an outline. It was all or nothing: the whole manuscript while it fit, then summaries and the last 3,000 characters. A chapter that already has chapters after it (one being rewritten) is given what follows too, and told to lead into it.
- **Requests are laid out for the providers' prefix caches.** What stays the same from one chapter to the next comes first — the book, the world, everybody in it, the outline of what came before, the text — and each new chapter only adds to its end; the chapter's own outline, where its people stand now and the brief come after. The window of text does not slide chapter by chapter: it holds its first chapter until the chapters no longer fit, then lets go of a quarter at once. The writer's standing instructions no longer carry the chapter's length. The usage ledger counts what a provider read from its cache, and prices it separately where a price is given.
- **A chapter's length is a recommendation, and there need be none.** A chapter may carry a recommended length, and so may the book; where neither does, the writer judges the length from what the chapter has to do (and is told how long the book's chapters have been running). New books are no longer stamped with 3,000 words a chapter, and that stamp on existing books no longer counts as a wish (`chapterBudget` in `novel.json` replaces `chapterWords`).
- **What happens on another line of the plot stays there.** Chapters that are not on the main line are digested like any other, but nobody is marked dead in the book for dying on a line, and no thread of the main line moves.

### Added

- **Batches: the same work for many, in one click.** One job does a set of steps for many chapters, characters or places; whatever can run side by side does; every step first looks whether anything is left to do, so a batch run again does what was left; a step that fails is the only thing that fails, and the batch says which. Three kinds: **the stage** (*Stage → Produce*: where every chapter stands — script, voices, sound, storyboard, pictures, video — and one button for what is missing; a chapter's voices, sounds and storyboard are made side by side once its script stands), **the text** (*Write → Write a run*: chapter after chapter, beats first where there are none and memory in between; or beats, memory and reviews for many chapters at once), **pictures and voices** (*Illustrations → Draw in a batch*: design sheets and place art first, then the portraits, chapter illustrations and cover drawn against them, and a voice per character). The listening desk makes a chapter's whole audio version — script, voices, sound — in one click, the motion-comic page the whole chapter. The showrunner has all of it as `novel_batch`.
- **The plot as a graph.** The outline's volumes are the main line; beside it there may be other lines — another way on the planner derived, an interlude played out, what the outline said before it was rewritten. A line leaves after a chapter and may arrive at one again. *Outline → Plot graph* draws them as tracks and stations and is where a line is opened, named, given the choice that leads onto it, joined to another, put aside, removed or **made the main line** (what the main line had in its place becomes a line; nothing is lost).
- **Rewrite, or go another way.** Deriving chapters now says where and how: at the end of a line, after any chapter, in place of what follows it (`replace` — chapters that are written are kept as a line put aside, never thrown away) or as a line of its own (`branch`). More than ten chapters are derived in rounds, up to 200 in one job. A what-if branch can be opened as a line.
- **Which way next.** The Write page lists one line at a time and switches between them; after a chapter where the story parts, it asks which line to go on with. `novel_write` takes a `line`. The reader follows a line too, and offers the choice at a fork — the words that were written down as what is decided there.
- **An interlude as a line.** What was played can be novelized as the first chapter of a line of its own, leaving where the scene branched off, with the player's first move as the choice.
- **Conversations go under the seal with their book.** DSH keeps every conversation as a plain log, and a conversation about a book holds that book. When a sealed book is locked, the conversations about it — the showrunner's, the runs of the roles, whatever a tool reached the book from — are encrypted to the book and taken out of DSH's store; when it is unlocked they are put back as they were. A sealed book's `novel.json` carries a public key for this (a drop), so it works while the book is locked; a conversation that is in use is sealed once it is let go, at the latest when DSH next starts.

### Changed

- **The crew are agents of DSH.** Continuity (场记, the `analyst` role), the planner (策划, `architect`), the director (导演) and the new post-production (后期, `producer`) are no longer prompts answered in one call: each is an agent with a persona, standing rules, skills, tools and permissions of its own, on DSH's own agent loop. A run is a **subagent of the conversation** that asked for the work — started through `ctx.subagents` by a provider this plugin registers — so it joins that conversation's composition, takes its permissions with approval pinned to "never", counts against the delegation depth, and is listed in the catalog of subagents in the conversation's header, where every step can be read with its tokens and time. Every place a pipeline asked a model for a JSON (digest, review, bible expansion, outline and beat derivation, what-if, foundation, the import's cast, world and volumes, audio scripts, storyboards, visual anchors, the scope of a book-wide revision) now hands the work to a role with tools to read the book and one tool to hand the result in with; what is handed in is validated on the spot and the turn ends there, so work whose material is complete still costs one request. A role that is set to plain calls, or whose agent run hands nothing in, answers in one call as before — and the job says which, and why. Jobs list the roles that worked in them, with a way into each run.
- **Text models are DSH's, and only DSH's.** Every text role runs on a model from DSH's *Settings → Models*; the Studio no longer connects text endpoints of its own, and the one-click setup fills only pictures, speech and retrieval. This is what lets every role be an agent, and it means a vendor is configured once. A role saved with an endpoint of its own by an earlier build keeps working by plain calls, and the Settings screen says so.
- Audio scripts, storyboards, visual anchors and illustration prompts are the director's work now (they were the analyst's); role names in the interface follow the crew: 场记, 策划, 导演, 后期.
- The Chinese interface calls the host DSH.

### Added

- **Who may use what.** *Settings → The crew*: per role, agent or plain calls; its own tools (the Studio's, plus the few of the conversation's that its work calls for — the planner searches the web, post-production has a shell and file tools) or all of DSH's; and a step budget. The preset now mounts a shell, job control and DSH's delegation tool for outside agents, so a role can be given them; the showrunner's own `novel_*` tools and the tools that need a person in the room are never a role's.
- **A task in words.** `novel_crew` hands a role a task that has no tool of its own — a question that takes reading and judging, research — and returns its report.
- **Skills per role.** Guides shipped under `crew/<role>/skills/` and registered for that role's runs alone: `novel-video-editing` for post-production, `novel-trend-research` for the planner.
- **A chapter's video, cut by an agent.** *Stage → Video* and `novel_video`: the chapter's voice, sound cues and storyboard pictures are laid out on one timeline — as JSON, and as one aligned voice track, subtitles, tables and a base script for a shell — and handed to post-production, which writes its own ffmpeg script, runs it, checks the result and hands in an MP4. The Studio only judges: a picture, a sound that is not silence, a length that matches the telling, no long black stretches. Outside help is DSH's own subagent: with `@deepseek-ai/dsh-subagent-claude-code` or `@deepseek-ai/dsh-subagent-codex` installed, post-production can hand the work on.
- **Settings that travel with a book.** *Model roles* and *The crew* are saved for every book or with the open one (its `novel.json` carries `models` and `crew`); `novel_settings action=book` does the same.
- **Sealing at creation.** A new book can be sealed as it is made: what is drafted or imported is ciphertext from its first file on.
- **A form that waits.** What is typed on the creation page is kept while the Studio is open: going to the settings and back no longer empties it.
- **What can be added can be taken away.** Chapters are deleted on the Write page; a line of an audio script and a whole script, a panel of a storyboard and a whole storyboard, a chapter's review and a chapter's video can each be removed — on their screens, and by the showrunner (`novel_manage action=delete_line | delete_script | delete_panel | delete_storyboard | delete_review`, `novel_video action=delete`).
- **Derive where you write.** The Write page asks the planner for the next chapters (reviewed before they join the outline) and for the open chapter's beats.

Text roles may use any provider the Harness already reaches — keyed or signed in, such as a ChatGPT subscription through Codex Connect — or call any OpenAI-compatible endpoint directly.
- **Keys.** Typed into the settings and saved with them, into the Harness credential store — one record per endpoint host, shared by every slot on that service, never written to the settings file and never sent back to the page. The usual environment variables are picked up by themselves.
- **Books in.** Import of `.txt`, `.md`, `.epub` and `.docx` from the entry page (drag and drop) or from the conversation, and creation of a new Harness workspace on the spot.
- **The book in play.** A conversation is on one book at a time and the Studio beside it shows the same one. Changing book on either side moves both; the showrunner is told, in the conversation's own record, which book is in play and which one earlier turns were about. Every tool takes an optional `book` to touch another one once, results say which book they are about once a conversation has been on several, and a call always acts on the book its model was last told about.
- **Usage and cost.** A ledger of every model call the Studio makes — tokens per role, pictures, speech — summed by model, role, book and day, and costed at the unit prices the author enters. Also available to the showrunner (`novel_settings action=usage`).
- **Said, not swallowed.** Work that needs images or speech before they are set up, or that fails for a missing key or sign-in, opens a prompt that leads to the one-click setup or to the right section of the settings — for jobs nobody was waiting on too. Voicing a chapter shows the lines done, the line in the booth, the time taken and the time left.
- **One page to read and to listen.** The reader shows a chapter two ways: as prose, and as an audio drama with the sentence being spoken lit in the text, any sentence a place to start from, and chapters following one another. The author's production desk for the audio version sits above the same page.
- **On a phone.** The reader can be served to other devices on the local network: off until switched on for a book, read-only, behind an address with a random token that can be replaced, shown as a link and as a code to scan.
- **One voice, one level.** A voice sheet per book so every speaker — with a card or without — keeps one voice across chapters and interludes; a listening check on every synthesized line (cut short, silent, padded, or not in its voice) with automatic retakes; all lines brought to one loudness; scripts that keep narration whole and say so when a passage could not be directed.
- **No cap of the Studio's own on what a model writes.** A model that reasons spends its reasoning out of the same allowance as its answer; replies run as long as the model allows unless a role sets `maxTokens`, and one cut off at a limit fails with that reason instead of being patched up.
- **Storyboards cut as fine as asked.** A density per book (sparse, balanced, dense, or left to the director) and a ceiling on panels; panels that may change in the middle of a long line; a director that is asked for the rest when it stops early.
- **The shelf.** Covers on the bookshelf and the overview, any illustration as cover, and removal of a book into the system trash — the book's own files only, nothing erased.
- **Nothing that looks like nothing.** Every button that starts work shows what it is doing until it ends, and a bar at the foot of the Studio lists everything in progress with a way to stop it.
- **A narrator who tells a story.** Narration that follows narration is spoken in one take (up to about 180 syllables, long paragraphs cut evenly at sentence ends), so the narrator is one person telling a story rather than a new voice at every paragraph. A "he said" stranded between two spoken lines is marked as a tag and left unspoken — as an audio drama does — unless the book is set to read every word; any line can be switched either way. Scripts cut the older, finer way are reshaped on request without calling a model, keeping the recordings of unchanged lines.
- **Sound design.** Besides its lines a script now says what each scene sounds like: the bed of the place, spot effects, music. Each cue has a caption the reader sees beside the text — in reading as in listening — and a description for a sound model. Effects, music and beds are generated by the speech setup's own model where that can make sound as well as speech (Bailian audio generation), or by an endpoint named for sound; without one the cues stay captions and beds are synthesized by the player. Every cue can be heard, reworded, made again, added or removed.
- **Listening opens on the script.** The view the author works in — who says what, how, over which sound — is what the listening mode shows first; the prose is one press away.
- **The ensemble knows its people.** A person on the lifecycle board, in the presence table or in the relationship graph opens their card. People the page names who have no card — gathered from chapter memories, suggestions, audio scripts and the voice sheet — are listed as walk-ons and settled one by one: a card of their own (keeping the voice and the lines they had), an alias of a known character, or off the list.
- **Faces in the cast list.** The character list shows everyone's portrait (or the front view of their design sheet) by default wherever there is one or a way to draw one, grouped into leads, supporting and minor parts, with one button to draw every missing portrait; a minimal mode shows names only.
- **Reading along with the model.** What a model thinks before and between its words — the chain of thought where the model shows it, the summary where it does not — is kept with every job and can be read live: above the prose as a chapter is written, and from the bar at the foot of the Studio for any other job.
- **The showrunner's own hands, and one road for every change.** `novel_edit` makes exact changes to a chapter — replace, insert, append, delete, set — for what is not writing: the author's own words, a name, a slip of fact. `novel_memory` corrects what the book remembers of a chapter. Every change by any tool keeps a version, digests memory again and goes on the book's record, shown on the overview with one-press undo for manuscript edits. Raw file tools aimed at a book's own files are refused with the name of the tool to use (or asked about, or let through, as the author sets); files changed outside the Studio all the same are noticed, recorded, and their chapters digested again.
- **A book on another device, to read or to change.** A shared book is read-only or — when the author chooses — changeable: its text can be edited from a phone (a version is kept, the change is on the record) and its interludes played there. The phone reader also plays the motion comic, shows the sound notes, and skips the lines left unspoken.
- **Sealed books.** A book can be sealed: every file of it — text, bible, outline, memory, pictures, audio, exports — is encrypted where it lies (AES-256-GCM), file names included. It opens with its password or with the computer's own unlock (Touch ID or the Mac's password on macOS; Windows Hello on Windows, untested), stays open until it is locked or the Harness quits, and is invisible to the showrunner while locked. A device reading it over the network shows the password once and is then remembered by a grant the author can withdraw.
- **An icon.** DeepSeek-chan, the whale-girl maid of the Chinese internet, reading a novel — drawn for this project. Character reference: 溟月 by 上善无形, in ZipZipPipe's DeepSeek maid design.
- **Import finds a book's real chapters — an editor that looks, on the Harness's own agent loop.** Chapters used to be found by rule (a heading alone on a line, else pieces of a set size), and a manuscript scraped from a web page — every heading glued to the site's navigation, one or two written differently — came out as two hundred numbered pieces. They are now found by an agent: an editor with eight tools over the text (`look`, `search`, `clean`, `split`, `amend`, `list`, `sections`, `finish`) that reads the opening, works out how the headings are written, removes what is not the book, splits, and reads the report of what came out — gaps in the numbering, pieces too short or too long, "titles" that are sentences of prose (told apart by the numbering into places where the text only mentions a chapter, and real headings with their first sentence on the same line, each with the correction to copy) — then puts it right and commits, with a note to the author on what it found. It never rewrites prose. Its steps scroll by in the import's progress; its reasoning is in the job's thought. Guard rails are the tools' own: a pattern that would delete a fifth of the book is refused, one that backtracks without end is cut off, committing over a sounder earlier split needs saying so, and an editor that runs out of steps leaves its best split behind rather than nothing. This is the first role that is an agent rather than a call: it is created through `ctx.agents` with a scope that holds only its persona and its tools, so requests, retries, tool calls, the session log, the token meter and cancellation are the Harness's (see *Roles as agents* in docs/design.md). Where no agent can run — a role set to an endpoint the Studio calls itself — the old rules stand in and the result says so.
- **Calls that fail in passing are made again.** The Harness retries a failed model request for its agent's turns and for nobody else: a call a plugin makes through the model seam used to end at the first busy server, rate limit or dropped stream. The Studio now runs the provider's own retry policy (from Settings → Models; five tries with a growing wait by default) for every call it makes, says so on the job's progress line while it waits, and counts every attempt in the usage ledger. A reply that completes with reasoning in it and no answer at all — which providers produce now and then, and which the adapters let through as a normal end — is treated the same way and asked for again, unchanged, up to three times; when a model spends its whole output allowance reasoning, that is said at once, with what to change. Failures of this kind are worded in the book's language.
- **Agent surface.** Twenty-four `novel_*` tools covering everything the Studio's screens can do — including its settings (`novel_settings`), showing a screen to the author (`novel_show`) and the small hand edits (`novel_manage`) — a project digest in the prompt, working modes, the `/novel` command, five workflow skills, tool rows that open the Studio, a Studio tab that opens by itself when the showrunner starts working and follows what it shows, and a confirmation gate for writes in read-only sessions, for removing a book and for opening one to the local network.
